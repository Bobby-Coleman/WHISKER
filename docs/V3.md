# Whisker v3: the rebuild

A small kitten and a wounded knight. A storybook adventure in the spirit of *It Takes Two*: two characters,
same pace, very different abilities, and every puzzle needs both. Single player switches between them (Tab /
Switch); the one you leave **stays exactly where you left it** (and keeps doing what it was doing: holding a
gate, standing on a plate) until you call it (Q / Call), when it follows you.

## Style

A toy-box storybook. Soft, rounded, chunky shapes; clean silhouettes; saturated but warm colour; soft shadows;
a painted gradient sky; stylised grass that moves in the wind. Nothing photographic. Think *It Takes Two*,
*Kirby and the Forgotten Land*, *A Short Hike* with more light. One consistent look across characters and world.

- **Materials.** `src/v3/render/materials.ts`. Everything is a `toy()` material: MeshStandardMaterial
  (high roughness, flat colour or vertex colour), with a soft wrap (light reaches a little past the terminator) and
  a faint rim light. Metal (the knight's plate, swords) is `metal()`: low roughness against a small sky env map.
  No textures except a few small tiling detail maps. No post-processing.
- **Palette** (`PAL` in materials.ts): meadow greens with yellow tips, warm earth path, slate-blue stone,
  dark-oak wood, banner reds and blues, cream and ginger kitten, steel-blue armour.
- **Scale.** Knight 1.8 m. Kitten about 0.34 m when she sits up, 0.2 m at the shoulder on all fours (5 : 1).
- **Characters.** Jointed-toy construction: each body part is a smooth rounded shape on a bone (like a wooden
  doll); joints overlap so no seams show. Exaggerated: the kitten has a big round head, huge glossy eyes, small
  pink nose, a pale pink bow; the knight has a great bucket helm, broad shoulders, big gauntlets and boots.

## Performance budget (phone first)

60 fps on a mid phone. Draw calls under 150, triangles under 400k on screen, JS under 4 ms a frame. Quality tiers
(low / medium / high) pick pixel ratio, shadow-map size and grass density; resolution adapts to frame time.
Instancing for grass, rocks, trees, fence posts; static scenery merged by material.

## Abilities

| Kitten | Knight |
|---|---|
| Climbs ivy and rough timber (ivy = climbable) | Too heavy to climb; mantles chest-high ledges |
| Swims; floats | Sinks: wades to his waist, deeper water sends him back |
| Fits through small holes, bars, drains, cat flaps | Pushes and pulls heavy blocks; lifts and holds gates |
| Light: blown about by the wind, safe on thin planks | Heavy: holds down plates, a windbreak, breaks rotten boards |
| Hits small switches and pulls pins | Turns cranks and winches; reaches high levers |
| | Lifts her and throws her up to ledges |

Same run speed and jump for both.

## Story beats

**Prologue, The Hawthorn** (dusk, the battlefield). A vast field after the battle, smoke columns, crows. One lone
hawthorn stands on a rise against the sunset. The wounded knight (a broken spear in his shoulder, blood on his
tabard) stumbles out of the haze toward it and slides down against its trunk. Far away, a kitten nudges her
mother, who will not wake; a horn sounds; she goes out into the field. Played: the kitten crosses the
battlefield toward the tree on the horizon (always visible), around and through wreckage, swimming a stream,
answering his breathing when she meows. She finds him. Cutscene: she hops up onto his leg, then his lap; he lifts
his head; muffled lines through the helm with subtitles; she curls up under his hand. Title. *Two summers later.*

**Chapter One, The Road to the Castle** (bright windy morning). The two of them (her in the small armour he made
her: breastplate, cream cape, pink bow) on the moor; the castle far off on its crag; a rider's summons. Play
across the moor toward the castle. Gates on the way (each a new pair of abilities):
1. *The Stone Wall.* A field wall, its gate tied from the far side. She climbs the ivy, drops over, unhooks it.
2. *The Sheep Pen / Ditch.* A gap too wide for her; he pushes a fallen trunk across it as a bridge.
3. *The Windy Ridge.* Gusts blow her off the ridge path unless she shelters behind rocks, or behind him: he is a
   movable windbreak. Place him in the gap between the rocks, then run her through in his lee.
4. *The Mill Stream.* The footbridge is broken. She swims across, climbs the mill, knocks the pin that drops the
   bridge's raised section. He crosses.
5. *The Castle Gate.* The drawbridge is up over the moat. She swims to the chain and climbs it into the
   gatehouse, drops the bridge. The portcullis: he heaves it up and holds it (and keeps holding when you switch
   away); she slips through the bars and throws the ratchet so it stays up. Into the bailey.

## Engine (src/v3)

- `main.ts` boot, loop, level loading in-page (no reloads), save of the reached chapter.
- `render/` look (sky, fog, lights, env), materials, quality tiers and adaptive resolution.
- `world/` terrain (height function → mesh + Rapier heightfield), grass, foliage, rocks, water, wind, castle kit.
- `physics.ts motor.ts climb.ts carry.ts follow.ts camera.ts timeline.ts game.ts` from v2, on plain `three`.
- `chars/` the toy kitten (quadruped procedural animation, sit, crouch, leap, swim, climb) and the toy knight
  (parts on the Quaternius UAL skeleton, its clips: walk, jog, push, carry, throw, hit, sit; a wounded stumble).
- `levels/` prologue, chapter one. Each a module: build, story director, puzzles.
- Tests: `tools/cap.mjs` (Linux Chromium, SwiftShader) with test plans; scripted bots play each level;
  every cutscene shot is checked for the camera inside a character or a wall.
