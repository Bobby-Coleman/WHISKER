# Whisker — story and world (draft for review)

A two-character journey in the spirit of *It Takes Two* and *Split Fiction*: a wounded knight and the kitten who
saved him cross the world to the castle, each doing what the other cannot. Played alone by switching between them
(a second player can join later). Linear chapters, each a new place with its own mechanic, set pieces and a boss.

Names are placeholders: **Sir Bram** (the knight) and **Whisker** (the kitten).

---

## Prologue — The Hawthorn (the opening)

Told like a Pixar short (*Kitbull*, *Piper*, *La Luna*): no words at all, a few minutes long, everything carried by
animation, light and music, with small moments of charm inside the sadness so the sadness lands. One visual motif,
the ribbon, set up here and paid off after the title.

Small beats that make it Pixar rather than grim:
- She sneezes at the smoke drifting off a dying fire, startles herself, and glares at the fire.
- Her first try at climbing him, she slides straight down his polished greave. She looks at the greave. She tries
  the knee instead.
- Halfway up she gets distracted by the ribbon on his sword hilt and bats at it, and he opens one eye.
- When she settles under his chin he lets out a breath that is half a laugh, and the rain on his gauntlet over her
  is the first warm thing in the scene.

No words. Rain, fog, and the sounds of the end of a battle.

1. **The Grey Moor, at dusk, after the battle.** Fog over the heather. Fires guttering in the rain. Broken banners,
   spears standing in the mud, a dropped helm, crows. (Nothing gory: the battle is told by what was left behind.)
2. **The knight.** Sir Bram staggers out of the mist, one hand pressed to his side where a blade went through his
   plate. He reaches a lone hawthorn on a rise and slides down against its trunk. He pulls off his helm. A faded pink
   ribbon is tied around the hilt of his sword: something of someone he lost long before this. He closes his eyes.
3. **The kitten.** By an overturned camp wagon, a tiny cream kitten nudges a still shape under a torn banner: her
   mother, the camp's cat, who will not wake. She curls against her for a moment. A horn sounds far off; she
   startles, and goes out alone into the fog, calling.
4. **Playable: the lost kitten.** You walk her through the wrecked field toward a faint sound of breathing: under a
   cart, over a fallen shield, up a broken spear shaft. The first steps of moving, jumping and climbing, learned the
   way she learns them.
5. **She finds him.** He opens his eyes. They look at each other: the end of his war, the beginning of her life alone.
6. **Playable: she climbs him.** Up his greave, his knee, the edge of his tasset, the dented breastplate. The
   climbing tutorial is his body.
7. **She stays.** She curls up in the hollow between his breastplate and his chin. He laughs once and it turns into a
   cough. He lifts his gauntlet and holds it over her, out of the rain. Her purring. His breathing. The night passes.
8. **Dawn.** Horns of the relief column, torches in the fog, voices calling for survivors. Fade to white.

**Title: WHISKER**

**Two summers later.** (The training is never shown.)

A watch-house on the moor's edge. Sir Bram, slower now, a hitch in his step, works at a small forge. The kitten
stands on the anvil in a suit of armour he made her from the plates of his own that were pierced that night: the
dent where the blade went in is now the crest on her breastplate. He ties the pink ribbon on her head. Play begins.

---

## Why they travel

The wound never healed right. Every winter it is worse, and he hides it from her. This spring the fever comes back,
and a rider brings word that the castle is calling every knight of the moor home. Bram sets out for the castle, and
he means to leave her there among the castle's knights, where she will be safe when he is gone. He does not tell her
that. She thinks they are going on an adventure.

The journey is his last one, and her first.

---

## Chapters

Each chapter: a place, a mechanic that uses their differences, a set piece, and a boss or climax. Both characters
can finish every chapter; they take different ways through it.

| | Chapter | Place | Mechanic | Set piece / boss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The Watch-House** | their home on the moor's edge | switching; he lifts, she climbs; the well bucket as a lift (she rides, he cranks) | packing for the road; the gate jammed by the storm |
| 2 | **The Grey Moor** | standing stones, heather, a broken windmill | wind: gusts carry the light kitten (her cape becomes a glider), his weight anchors her; mill sails as moving platforms | the storm front; boss: the great crow that nests in the windmill |
| 3 | **The Drowned Marsh** | reed beds, sluices, a sunken village | water level: sluice wheels raise and drain the water; he wades, she rides floating things and his shoulders | boss: the old heron of the marsh |
| 4 | **The Old Battlefield** | where it began, overgrown now | siege engines as launchers and lifts; rust-locked machinery he can force and she can unjam from inside | she finds the overturned wagon. A quiet chapter. Boss: an empty suit of armour that will not stop fighting |
| 5 | **The Bell Abbey** | a clockwork bell tower on a crag | time: pendulums, gears and bells as platforms; ringing changes the tower | the tower turns as it rings; boss: the clockwork guardian |
| 6 | **The Castle** | ramparts, kitchens, the great hall | Bram collapses at the gates. **You can no longer switch.** She goes on alone with only her own abilities, to ring the great bell and bring the healers | the loss of the second character is the climax |
| | **Epilogue — The Hawthorn** | the castle garden, or back on the moor | | she on his chest again, under a hawthorn, in the sun |

The ending is the one decision left open: he lives, retired with a cane, and she becomes the knight of the watch
(hopeful, as *It Takes Two* ends); or he dies under the tree with her on his chest, as she found him (bittersweet).

---

## What each can do

They run at the same pace and jump the same distance (every gap is crossable by both). They differ in what else
they can do, so each chapter has her way and his way through it, and places that need both.

**Whisker (kitten):** climbs walls with ivy, rough stone, rope and timber; squeezes through drains, hatches and gaps;
walks beams too weak for him; light, so wind carries her and her cape glides; can ride on his shoulders or shield.

**Sir Bram (knight):** lifts and throws her up to ledges; holds what is too heavy (gates, portcullises, falling
beams); breaks rotten boards and rusted locks; heavy enough for pressure plates and counterweights; wades deep
water; his shield blocks what would knock her off (wind, arrows, fire).

---

## The look

The fog, the grass and the light stay. Everything built gets a stylized hand-made look in the spirit of *It Takes
Two* and Pixar's shorts: appealing, softly bevelled shapes, stones and timbers with a little irregularity, colour
carried by the models rather than photographs, moss and wear painted in, and lighting doing the emotional work. The
characters get the same treatment: a plush, big-eyed, expressive kitten and a stocky, gentle-looking knight about
three times her height, built to act (faces and poses that read from across the moor).
